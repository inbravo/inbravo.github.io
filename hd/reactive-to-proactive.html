Reactive to Proactive — How Six Firms Changed Their Commercial Motion

Each firm started as a delivery-first, inbound-dependent consultancy. The transformation into a high-growth engine happened when they changed how they generated demand — not just how they delivered. This maps the specific before/after states and the documented triggers for each company.

Five cross-company patterns

01

Platform award stage replaces cold outreach

A Partner of Year win in a specific vertical category — not a general award — means every enterprise buyer researching that platform's ecosystem finds the firm before the firm calls them. The platform's own field team starts referring deals.

Tredence: 4 consecutive Databricks Retail & CPG awards. Tiger: Snowflake Elite in 10 months. Quantiphi: 21 Google Cloud PoY wins starting pre-Series A. None required PE capital — only certifications and named accelerators.

02

Skip the CEO — find the functional buyer

The CEO is a close, not an entry. The CMO, CRO, Chief Risk Officer, or VP Analytics owns the problem, controls the budget line, and can commit. LatentView made this pivot explicitly before its IPO and credits it for the quality of its managed-services base.

LatentView: redirected all outreach from CEO to functional leaders (pre-IPO, bootstrapped). Fractal: CXO hunter roles target named buyers by function, not seniority. This requires knowing which functional leader feels the pain — a vertical knowledge problem, not a headcount problem.

03

External commercial hire formalises the shift

The first non-founder CCO, CRO, or commercial VP is the org-chart signal of proactive GTM. Before this hire, commercial reach is bounded by the founders' personal networks. After it, hunting becomes systematic and independent of any single relationship.

Sigmoid: Shankar Viswanathan (P&G, 30 yrs) as CCO, October 2023, year 10. Quantiphi: Jim Reesing (Accenture) as CEO, 2026. LatentView: CGO + 12 GTM hires, FY23. Tredence: COO Singh + CBO Imrapur, 2024–25. In every case, founders moved to client strategy and stopped carrying pipeline.

04

Analyst placement creates structural inbound

A Forrester Wave or Everest Group PEAK Matrix mention puts the firm into every enterprise buyer's research stack — without the firm initiating contact. This is not a marketing campaign; it is infrastructure. One analyst mention compounds for 18–24 months.

Tiger Analytics: Forrester Wave appearance in 2023 correlated with the firm's fastest growth phase. Sigmoid: Forrester AI Consultancies and Everest Group placements arrived alongside CCO hire. LatentView: Gartner and ISG recognition post-IPO opened European enterprise procurement. Submittal and briefing process requires 1–2 completed reference engagements, not capital.

05

ICP discipline makes specialisation visible

No firm announced "we are a specialist." The specialisation shows in deal patterns — which accounts they pursued, which they declined, where their certifications are. Buyers in the target vertical notice the concentration and self-select. ICP filters create the appearance of specialisation that draws the right buyers in.

Fractal: 10-20-30 ICP filter, defined before TA Associates' $25M. Tiger: 90% of revenue in 4 verticals with no documented client refusals. Sigmoid: 50 people per client on average — ratio itself signals non-commodity positioning. In every case, the filter predates the growth phase it enabled. The work of declining wrong-fit accounts is not visible to the market; only the resulting concentration is.
